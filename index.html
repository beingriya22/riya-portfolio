01 / Latest work (selected)

I was handed four problems, and none of them was the product.

MonoCloud is a young B2B identity and auth platform going up against Auth0 and Clerk. When I joined, four things were unsettled: almost nobody knew it existed; the positioning read like a login company when the product does far more than login; the ICP was aimed at developers when the people signing off were decision makers; and there was no audience to launch into. The product was solid. Everything around it was open.

Since then: competitive teardowns of Auth0, Clerk, Kinde and Scalekit, a repositioning around agent and machine identity that now anchors the homepage, a Product Hunt launch that reached top 7 organically and grew the page to 336+ followers, an outreach framework the team runs as its standard motion, and content across seven channels structured so AI answer engines cite it too.

05 / How I work

Four habits, learned the expensive way.

Read the product before writing about it

For technical products, credibility is the whole funnel. If I can't explain the mechanism to an engineer, I'm not ready to explain the value to a buyer.

Support tickets are free research

Every repeat ticket is a sentence that's missing from the product. Reading the queue is cheaper than a survey and more honest than one.

Positioning is a subtraction exercise

Teams lose to competitors by describing themselves in the competitor's vocabulary. The work is finding the one claim only you can make, then cutting the rest.

Build the thing the team can run without me

A framework someone else uses in month three beats a campaign I ran perfectly once. Anything I build is meant to outlast my involvement in it.
